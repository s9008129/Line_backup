## Strongest counter-argument considered (and why it does not flip the verdict)

The physical anchors that pin the final native hop are partially *self-referential*: the software-cursor overlay and glyph are drawn by the same harness whose click semantics they are used to validate, so they prove the harness's *intended* point, not necessarily the OS-delivered point; the only non-self-referential anchor (the ellipsis click opening the popup) has a control-sized, not pixel-level, tolerance, and the last hop is a compiled binary. If the native layer had (hypothetically) delivered the Save-All click somewhere else while still drawing the overlay at the intended point, the hit-test would be wrong despite all harness anchors. Weighed: (a) every discrete alternative mapping family is excluded by ≥420 px (Save-All glyph) and ≥90 pt (ellipsis semantics) — far beyond any plausible native drift; (b) the one independent (app-behaviour) anchor pins the origin/scale family; (c) the worst-case anchor interpretation still leaves ≥4.2 pt to the nearest band edge; (d) this exact limitation is disclosed as residual R1. The residual is therefore bounded and disclosed — it cannot flip the CONFIRMED bar, which is exactly what the round claims.
